Retraining Strategy Comparison (Slide 5 Direct Empirical Evidence)

Approach B: Static (No Retrain) Repeated Crashes
Model preserves baseline training weights but remains 100% blind to newly emerged DDoS vectors. Miss rate (FNR) spikes to 85%+, resulting in repeated server outages.
Crashes: 6-8 Accuracy: ~62.1% Recall: ~15.0%
Approach A: Retrain on New Only Catastrophic Forgetting
Model quickly updates on recent crash data but forgets baseline traffic patterns. Hyper-shifts decision boundary, causing false alarms on normal traffic.
Crashes: 1 Accuracy: ~98.1% Stability: Unstable
Our Approach: Balanced Mixture Proposed Winner
Combines newly harvested crash DDoS vectors + recent benign samples + historical baseline data. Adapts to new attacks while completely preserving past stability.
Crashes: 1 (Resolved) Accuracy: 100% Recall: 100%

Jury Defense Guide: What to Highlight During the Review

1. Research Gap (Slide 3)

No prior work uses confirmed server crashes as retroactive ground truth. Traditional pipelines require human labeling, which cannot prevent downtime.

2. Closed Loop (Slide 2)

When false negatives overwhelm the server, the failure acts as an automatic external supervisor, marking recent unclassified traffic as DDoS.

3. ADWIN Isolation (Slide 4)

ADWIN dynamically checks Hoeffding bounds on historical sub-windows to isolate the exact change-point timestamp where traffic parameters drifted.

4. Balanced Mixture (Slide 5)

Retraining on old + new data mixtures guarantees that the classifier stops the new mutated attack without catastrophic forgetting of classic attacks.
